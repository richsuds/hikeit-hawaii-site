Pīpīwai Trail

Plan Pīpīwai Trail in Maui’s Kīpahulu District: a 4-mile round-trip forest hike, waterfall viewpoints, route milestones and official access checks.

Choose the full hike or a shorter turnaround

NPS describes a moderately strenuous, 4-mile round trip with 800 feet of elevation change. Makahiku overlook is about half a mile in, bamboo about a mile in, and the Waimoku viewing area about two miles in. These are one-way milestones. Budget for the return from whichever point you choose; reaching a landmark is only part of the outing.

Fit Pīpīwai into your Road to Hāna day

This is in coastal Kīpahulu, beyond Hāna, rather than the Haleakalā summit district. Plan the drive, walk and return together. If earlier stops leave too little daylight or energy, shorten the outing instead of rushing to the waterfall. Bring food, water and the supplies you need before reaching the park; food and fuel are not sold there.

What to expect underfoot

NPS describes forest, boardwalks and footbridges along the route. Humid, rainy conditions make footwear with grip and a flexible turnaround plan useful. Keep your attention on footing even when the scenery opens up. Stay on marked routes and obey the barriers at viewpoints; a waterfall photograph is not a reason to continue beyond a closure.

Waterfall views are not a swimming plan

Treat this as a walk to viewpoints. NPS warns of flash floods and sudden rockfall in Kīpahulu. Its conditions page, checked November 4, 2026, says the Pools of ʻOheʻo are not accessible for swimming. Recheck current alerts before departure; no live water-level or trail-safety assessment is implied here.

Access: published guidance and today’s status

The official hiking page identifies the route, but this page does not independently confirm that it is open today. Check NPS alerts and posted signs, and review park fees before travel. Summit sunrise reservations and coastal Kīpahulu planning are different matters; use the official guidance for the district you intend to visit.
