Oʻahu Hikes That Require Reservations, Permits or Passes

A reservation, hiking permit, approval letter and parking pass are different things. Use this guide to identify the correct requirement for your route, then complete it through the official land manager before committing travel time.

Official sources reviewed November 1, 2026. Planning guidance; recheck access and conditions before travel.

Timed reservation • nonresidents

Diamond Head Summit Trail

Estimated hiking time: 1½–2 hours round trip.

State Parks guidance for the designated summit route and return. State Parks time guidance.

This is the most straightforward visitor booking on the list. Non-Hawaiʻi residents must reserve entry, and reserve parking too if they plan to use the monument lot.

Access to plan around

Reservations are separate from trail conditions. Normal hours are 6 am–6 pm, last entrance is 4:30 pm for the final reservation, and all visitors must leave by closing.

Private-land hiking permit • restricted

Lāʻie Falls Trail

Estimated hiking time: Full-route time not shown while access is restricted.

The manager says not to hike past Pine Forest because of landslide damage. A historical time to the falls would not describe the currently permitted outing. Check current route restrictions.

Hawaiʻi Reserves requires an active online hiking permit for this privately owned route. The permit records permission; it does not remove current route restrictions.

Access to plan around

Hawaiʻi Reserves currently says landslide damage affects the trail past Pine Forest and instructs hikers not to continue beyond that point. Do not treat the permit as permission to reach the falls while that restriction remains.

DLNR day-use hiking permit

Lulumahu Falls Trail

Estimated hiking time: Time not yet verified.

A reliable duration for the permitted route has not been confirmed. Wet footing and stream conditions can make travel slower or require turning back. Official route and permit information.

DLNR identifies Lulumahu Falls as a Honolulu Watershed route that needs a permit for access. Apply through the official day-use permit portal rather than relying on an unofficial directions page.

Access to plan around

The route includes wet forest and stream hazards. A permit is access authorization, not a statement that water levels, footing or weather are safe that day.

DLNR day-use permit • vehicle rules

Kuaokalā Trail

Estimated hiking time: Time not yet verified.

Confirm your entry point and return route first. Approaches through the tracking station and longer connecting routes should not share one estimate. Official access information.

DLNR lists Kuaokalā among the Oʻahu hikes with online day permits. Vehicle access and camping are separate questions and may use different authorizations.

Access to plan around

Confirm whether your plan includes vehicle access through the tracking-station road or camping at Peacock Flats. Do not assume one authorization covers every activity.

Poamoho Trail

Estimated hiking time: Time not yet verified.

A trailhead-to-summit estimate must be kept separate from the remote road approach. Confirm current access and your return plan before choosing a departure time. Official access information.

Written Army approval • 10 business days

Schofield–Waikāne Trail

Estimated hiking time: Time not yet verified.

The Army describes a 14-mile round trip for intermediate and expert hikers but does not publish a completion time. Plan the full outing within your written access authorization and daylight. Army route and access guidance.

The Army describes this as a 14-mile round trip for intermediate and expert hikers. Access requires an approved written request rather than an instant online hiking pass.

Access to plan around

Submit a signed request at least 10 business days before the requested weekend hike. The Army requires the approval letter to be carried during the outing and provides installation access procedures after approval.

Limited vehicle parking pass • not a hiking permit

Wiliwilinui Ridge Trail

Estimated hiking time: 2½–4 hours round trip.

The access manager’s estimate starts at the trailhead. Add the gate-to-trailhead walk and return if parking outside the community or using rideshare. Access manager’s time guidance.

Hikers may enter the gated Waialae Iki 5 community from sunrise to sunset, but automobile parking passes are limited. Once the passes run out, drivers must park outside the gate where legally permitted.

Access to plan around

Ride-share drop-off and pickup are outside the gate, adding about one mile of walking to the trailhead. Motorcycles, mopeds, motorized bikes and commercial vehicles are prohibited from entering for trail access.

Know which permission you need

Diamond Head uses timed entry and parking reservations for nonresidents. Hawaiʻi Reserves and DLNR issue route-specific hiking permits. The Army requires advance written approval for Schofield–Waikāne. Wiliwilinui limits vehicle parking passes but does not describe the pass as a hiking permit.

Permission does not override a closure

A permit or reservation does not guarantee that every section is open, safe or reachable. Check the manager’s current notice after receiving permission and again shortly before departure. Follow the most restrictive current instruction.

Match the authorization to the route and date

Confirm the exact trail, entry point, vehicle requirement, names in the party and permitted date. Keep the reservation, permit or approval available during the outing. Do not substitute a camping, hunting or commercial-use permit for a hiking authorization.

Check again before you go

Hours and reservations are planning facts, not a live opening confirmation. Check State Parks notices, the National Weather Service Honolulu forecast and the manager’s page for your destination.

Temporary notices reviewed November 1, 2026: State Parks lists storm-recovery closures and facility impacts around Hawaiʻi, including an ʻAiea Loop restroom closure through November 25. Makapuʻu Lookout repair work is separate from the Lighthouse Trail. Recheck the official notice because listed end dates are not reopening guarantees.
