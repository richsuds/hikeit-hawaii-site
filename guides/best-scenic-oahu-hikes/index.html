Best Scenic Oʻahu Hikes for Visitors

Coastal panorama • paved climb

Makapuʻu Point Lighthouse Trail

HikeIt difficulty: Easy. Paved with a sustained uphill grade, about 500 feet of gain and little shade. Heat and wind add effort. State Parks rates the route Moderate.

Estimated hiking time: About 2 hours round trip.

State Parks allows time to enjoy the views on the main paved route. Closed tide pools and the lighthouse are not part of this outing. State Parks time guidance.

A 2-mile round-trip paved route climbs about 500 feet above Oʻahu’s southeastern coast. It is a strong choice for broad ocean views when the group is prepared for a sustained, exposed uphill walk.

Access to plan around

Kaiwi publishes seasonal hours beginning at 7 am and lists no drinking water or restrooms. The tide pools are in a closed, dangerous area and are not part of this recommendation.

Crater and coast • reservations

Diamond Head Summit Trail

Estimated hiking time: 1½–2 hours round trip.

State Parks guidance for the designated summit route and return. State Parks time guidance.

The summit route pairs crater geology and military history with expansive south-shore views. Expect steep sections, stairs, a tunnel and an exposed return rather than a gentle overlook walk.

Access to plan around

Nonresidents need entry and parking reservations. Normal hours are 6 am–6 pm, with the last entrance at 4:30 pm for the final reservation; all visitors must leave by closing.

Forest and ridge • longer outing

ʻAiea Loop Trail

Estimated hiking time: 2½–3 hours for the loop.

State Parks guidance for the full 4.8-mile loop. Mud, roots and the return walk to your vehicle can slow your group. State Parks time guidance.

A forested loop with ridge openings offers a different landscape from the dry coastal routes. Choose it when the group is ready for a longer natural-surface hike and potentially muddy footing.

Access to plan around

Check Keaīwa Heiau State Recreation Area notices before travel. State Parks currently lists all three restrooms closed for construction through November 25, 2026; that date is not a reopening guarantee.

Choose the view and the effort together

Makapuʻu offers a paved coastal climb, Diamond Head combines a crater summit with reservations and stairs, and ʻAiea Loop offers a longer forest route with changing footing. Compare the full return trip, not only the photograph at the viewpoint.

Scenic does not mean low risk

Open viewpoints can add heat, UV and wind exposure. Forest routes may stay wet after the coast dries. Carry water, keep to the established route and choose a different outing when official closures or weather conflict with the plan.

Use a current conditions check

Look at the forecast for the trail area, recent rain, official park notices and the trail manager’s page shortly before departure. Visitor popularity does not establish that a route is open or suitable today.

Check again before you go

Hours and reservations are planning facts, not a live opening confirmation. Check State Parks notices, the National Weather Service Honolulu forecast and the manager’s page for your destination.

Temporary notices reviewed November 1, 2026: State Parks lists storm-recovery closures and facility impacts around Hawaiʻi, including an ʻAiea Loop restroom closure through November 25. Makapuʻu Lookout repair work is separate from the Lighthouse Trail. Recheck the official notice because listed end dates are not reopening guarantees.
