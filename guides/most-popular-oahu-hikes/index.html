Most Popular Oʻahu Hikes for Visitors

These are widely recognized visitor outings, not a live attendance ranking. Each offers a different version of Oʻahu—from crater and coastal views to rainforest, steep stairs and a managed cultural valley—so choose by fit rather than fame alone.

COMPARE YOUR OPTIONS

Five popular outings compared

Iconic crater • reservations

Diamond Head Summit Trail

Choose Diamond Head for a recognizable summit, crater geology, military history and south-shore views. The short mileage still includes steep stairs, a tunnel and exposed sections.

Access to plan around

Non-Hawaiʻi residents need advance entry reservations and a separate parking reservation if using the monument lot. Normal hours are 6 am–6 pm and all visitors must leave by closing.

Rainforest • waterfall

Mānoa Falls Trail

Choose Mānoa Falls for a lush Honolulu Watershed setting and a designated route to a waterfall viewpoint. Expect wet terrain to behave differently from the coast even on the same day.

Access to plan around

DLNR identifies Mānoa Falls among the popular Honolulu Watershed trails. Stay on the maintained route, remain outside closed areas and never use waterfall appearance as proof that stream conditions are safe.

Coastal views • paved climb

Makapuʻu Point Lighthouse Trail

Choose Makapuʻu for sweeping southeastern-coast views on a paved 2-mile round trip. The route climbs about 500 feet and remains hot, dry, windy and exposed.

Access to plan around

Kaiwi publishes seasonal hours beginning at 7 am and lists no drinking water or restrooms. The lighthouse is off-limits, and the dangerous tide-pool area is closed.

Steep workout • experienced hikers

Koko Crater Railway Trail

Choose Koko Crater for a strenuous railway ascent rather than an easy sightseeing walk. The steep climb and descent demand fitness, careful footing and enough water for exposed conditions.

Access to plan around

Koko Crater is City-managed. Koko Head District Park is closed from 11 pm to 4 am; that schedule does not guarantee parking, trail conditions or suitability for a low-light ascent.

Managed valley • paid admission

Waimea Valley Falls Walk

Choose Waimea Valley for a paved main path through botanical gardens and Hawaiian cultural sites to the waterfall area, with facilities and optional paid shuttle service.

Access to plan around

Admission, seasonal hours and parking fees apply. Waterfall access and swimming can close because of rain or high flow, and swimming is never guaranteed by an admission ticket.

Popular does not mean similar

Diamond Head requires reservations for nonresidents. Mānoa Falls is a wet forest walk. Makapuʻu is a paved but exposed coastal climb. Koko Crater is a steep railway ascent. Waimea Valley is a paid, managed garden and cultural-site visit. Compare the actual outing before choosing.

Expect crowds and plan the approach

Well-known routes can have busy entrances, limited parking and slower sections. Check reservations, opening hours, legal parking and the time needed to return. Arriving early does not authorize entry before the manager’s published opening time.

Use conditions to break the tie

When several routes fit your interests, compare rain, mud, heat, wind, UV and official notices. A famous waterfall route may be a poor choice after heavy rain, while a dry coastal climb can still be difficult in heat and strong wind.

Check again before you go

Hours and reservations are planning facts, not a live opening confirmation. Check State Parks notices, the National Weather Service Honolulu forecast and the manager’s page for your destination.
