SUNSET

Oʻahu Sunset Hikes & Viewpoints

The best sunset outing for a visitor may be a viewpoint close to the return transport. A summit reached at sunset usually leaves the descent after dark, so separate an afternoon hike from the place where you watch the final light.

Official sources reviewed November 1, 2026. Planning guidance; recheck access and conditions before travel.

Lookout • seasonal exit time

Puʻu ʻUalakaʻa State Wayside

Visit timing: Flexible viewpoint visit; no fixed hiking time.

This card describes the lookout, not the nearby forest loop. Budget separately for any daylight trail walk and leave before the park closes. Official viewpoint information.

Consider the lookout for late-day views over Honolulu. Treat the nearby forest trail as a separate daylight activity; the loop is not the sunset viewpoint.

Access to plan around

Published hours are 7 am–7:45 pm in summer and 7 am–6:45 pm in winter. Winter begins the day after Labor Day; summer begins April 1. Check parking fees and current signs.

Afternoon hike • leave before closing

Diamond Head Summit Trail

Estimated hiking time: 1½–2 hours round trip.

State Parks guidance for the designated summit route and return. State Parks time guidance.

Choose an afternoon crater walk only when your reservation leaves ample time to descend and exit. Do not assume you can stay at the summit for sunset.

Access to plan around

The 6 pm gate closing time governs the visit even when sunset is later. Confirm current entry rules and allow time for the full return.

Non-hike sunset alternative

Ala Moana / Magic Island

Consider a park walk and viewpoint when a mountain outing would leave too little daylight for the return. Check the actual western horizon from your chosen spot and stay out of the water unless you have separately assessed ocean conditions.

Access to plan around

The City lists Ala Moana Regional Park hours as 4 am–10 pm. Verify notices, parking rules and your return transport; this is a park outing, not a hiking-trail recommendation.

Official access information

Look west, then check the actual horizon

The sun sets on the western side of the sky, shifting north or south with the season. A west-facing view can still be blocked by a ridge or clouds. Choose the specific viewpoint for your date; a trail on the west side of the island is not automatically a good sunset route.

Work backward from the exit

Use the earlier of the park closing time and the time you need to be off the trail in daylight. Include breaks, slower downhill walking and the walk to your vehicle. Set a turnaround alarm before starting. Carry a light for delays, but do not use it as a reason to plan an unfamiliar trail descent in darkness.

Keep a viewpoint option in reserve

If a hike cannot fit the daylight window, shorten it or skip it and choose a permitted viewpoint with a straightforward exit. If the gate closes before the sunset you want to see, choose another outing. Do not move past a barrier, park on a prohibited shoulder or use a closed route for a photograph.

Check again before you go

Hours and reservations are planning facts, not a live opening confirmation. Check State Parks notices, the National Weather Service Honolulu forecast and the manager’s page for your destination.

Temporary notices reviewed November 1, 2026: State Parks lists storm-recovery closures and facility impacts around Hawaiʻi, including an ʻAiea Loop restroom closure through November 25.
