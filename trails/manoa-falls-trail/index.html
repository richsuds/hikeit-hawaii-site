Official trail information

Arrival and parking

The trail is reached near the end of Mānoa Road. Lyon Arboretum’s lot is reserved for arboretum visitors, not Mānoa Falls hikers. Its official directions identify paid parking at Paradise Park nearby. Verify current lot hours, charges and posted parking restrictions before travel; a mapped route point is not verified parking.

Plan your visit

The catalog lists the 0.8-mile segment from the end of Mānoa Road to the falls. About 1.6 miles is HikeIt’s estimate for returning along the same segment, before any parking or bus-stop approach. Confirm the current route with Nā Ala Hele.

Plan a forest walk to the falls

Mānoa offers a rainforest setting rather than a crater panorama. Allow for wet steps, roots and muddy footing even when Waikīkī is sunny. Stay on the maintained route, follow barriers and closures, and treat the falls as a viewing destination. Water appearance and flow estimates do not establish that swimming or stream crossings are safe.

DLNR Oʻahu trail information and alerts

Parking: do not use the arboretum lot

Lyon Arboretum reserves its parking for arboretum visitors and says Mānoa Falls hikers parked there will be towed. Its directions page identifies a paid Paradise Park lot nearby. Confirm current availability, hours and charges with the lot operator, and obey signs. Do not park on the access road or assume a map pin marks legal parking.

Consider Route 5, including the approach walk

TheBus Route 5 connects Ala Moana with Mānoa/Kumuone. Lyon’s official directions describe a walk beyond the final stop toward Paradise Park and the arboretum, passing the Mānoa Falls trailhead. That is neighborhood approach information, not a measured trailhead walk. Check your Waikīkī connection, current stops, pedestrian access and return service before leaving.

Keep the rest of the day flexible

Add your approach and return travel to the hiking-time estimate above. Carry your water rather than relying on facilities being open. If rain, access notices or your group’s footing make the outing unsuitable, change the plan instead of searching for an unofficial waterfall shortcut.

Plan for the terrain

Water levels can change quickly after rain, including rain upstream. Do not enter fast-moving water or rely on a waterfall-flow estimate to judge a crossing.
