Makapuʻu Point Lighthouse Trail

Plan Makapuʻu Point Lighthouse Trail with its official route map, difficulty, access details, weather, UV, rainfall, and estimated mud and water-flow conditions.

Also known as: Makapuu Lighthouse Trail · Makapuʻu Lighthouse Hike · Kaiwi Trail

Trail facts

  • Region: East Honolulu
  • Difficulty: Moderate
  • Length: 2 miles
  • Elevation gain: 500 feet
  • Managed by: Hawaiʻi DLNR — Division of State Parks
  • Trail type: STANDARD
  • Access status: VERIFY

Estimated hiking time

About 2 hours round trip

State Parks allows time to enjoy the views on the main paved route. Closed tide pools and the lighthouse are not part of this outing. State Parks time guidance.

Planning estimate, not a measured average. Add time for parking, breaks and your group’s pace. Sources checked 2026-11-07.

Trail features

Lighthouse views, Coastal views, Whale watching, Historic site

Amenities

Parking, interpretive signs, lookouts

Route orientation

Listed start: Kaiwi State Scenic Shoreline parking area. Listed end: Makapuʻu Head lookouts. Confirm the actual route and return path with the land manager's current map.

Mapped hazards

High UV, heat, wind, cliff edges

Access planning

Check State Parks notices before visiting.

Official trail information

Arrival and parking

Driving directions are unavailable until a road-accessible arrival point is verified. A mapped route point is not verified parking. Check the land manager’s entry, parking and transport instructions before travel.

Official arrival and access instructions

Plan for the terrain

Recent rain can leave steps, roots and rock slick after skies clear. Consider heat, shade, footing and the return trip as well as the forecast.

The listed 2-mile length does not account for detours, rests or the time needed to return. Access has not been verified; confirm the current status with the land manager.

Check before you leave

  1. Read the latest National Weather Service forecast and alerts for the trail area.
  2. Confirm the manager’s current access and permit rules, including the intended entry point.
  3. Share your route and expected return time with someone, and choose a lower-exposure alternative if conditions change.
