Waimea Valley Falls Walk

Plan Waimea Valley Falls Walk with its official route map, difficulty, access details, weather, UV, rainfall, and estimated mud and water-flow conditions.

Also known as: Waimea Falls · Waimea Valley Trail · Waimea Falls Trail

Trail facts

  • Region: North Shore
  • Difficulty: Easy, paved and accessible
  • Length: 1.5 miles
  • Managed by: Waimea Valley
  • Trail type: WATERFALL
  • Access status: RESTRICTED

Estimated hiking time

About 40–60 minutes walking round trip

HikeIt doubles the operator’s 20–30 minute one-way walk, assuming a similar return pace. Allow 2–3 hours for a fuller valley visit, including gardens and cultural activities; swimming is not guaranteed. This estimate does not grant access. Meet current reservation, admission or permit requirements and confirm the permitted route before visiting. Waimea Valley visit guidance.

Planning estimate, not a measured average. Add time for parking, breaks and your group’s pace. Sources checked 2026-11-07.

Trail features

Waterfall, Botanical garden, Cultural sites, Accessible path

Amenities

Restrooms, food, shuttle, cultural and botanical interpretation

Route orientation

Listed start: Waimea Valley entrance. Listed end: Waimea Falls. Confirm the actual route and return path with the land manager's current map.

Mapped hazards

Heat, rain; waterfall access may close for high flow

Access planning

Paid admission required; check hours and waterfall access before visiting.

Arrival and parking

Driving directions are unavailable until a road-accessible arrival point is verified. A mapped route point is not verified parking. Check the land manager’s entry, parking and transport instructions before travel.

Official arrival and access instructions

Plan for the terrain

Water levels can change quickly after rain, including rain upstream. Do not enter fast-moving water or rely on a waterfall-flow estimate to judge a crossing.

The listed 1.5-mile length does not account for detours, rests or the time needed to return. Check for new closures or restrictions before travel.

Check before you leave

  1. Read the latest National Weather Service forecast and alerts for the trail area.
  2. Confirm the manager’s current access and permit rules, including the intended entry point.
  3. Share your route and expected return time with someone, and choose a lower-exposure alternative if conditions change.
