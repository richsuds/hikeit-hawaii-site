Aiea Loop Trail

Plan Aiea Loop Trail with its official route map, difficulty, access details, weather, UV, rainfall, and estimated mud and water-flow conditions.

Trail facts

  • Region: Central Oʻahu
  • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate
  • Length: 4.8 miles
  • Elevation gain: 900 feet
  • Managed by: Hawaiʻi DLNR — Division of State Parks
  • Trail type: STANDARD
  • Access status: VERIFY

Estimated hiking time

2½–3 hours for the loop

State Parks guidance for the full 4.8-mile loop. Mud, roots and the return walk to your vehicle can slow your group. State Parks time guidance.

Planning estimate, not a measured average. Add time for parking, breaks and your group’s pace. Sources checked 2026-11-07.

Keaīwa Heiau restroom construction notice

State Parks’ November 7 notice lists all three restrooms at Keaīwa Heiau State Recreation Area closed for construction through November 25, 2026. This is a restroom notice, not a trail-closure notice. The listed end date is not a reopening guarantee; recheck the official notice before visiting.

State Parks notices · Checked November 8, 2026 (HST).

Route orientation

Listed start: Upper eastern end of Keaīwa Heiau State Recreation Area park road. Listed end: Lower campground; follow the park road back to the trailhead. Confirm the actual route and return path with the land manager's current map.

Access planning

Confirm current access, permits and closures with the land manager before visiting.

Arrival and parking

Driving directions are unavailable until a road-accessible arrival point is verified. A mapped route point is not verified parking. Check the land manager’s entry, parking and transport instructions before travel.

Official arrival and access instructions

Plan for the terrain

Recent rain can leave steps, roots and rock slick after skies clear. Consider heat, shade, footing and the return trip as well as the forecast.

The listed 4.8-mile length does not account for detours, rests or the time needed to return. Access has not been verified; confirm the current status with the land manager.

Check before you leave

  1. Read the latest National Weather Service forecast and alerts for the trail area.
  2. Confirm the manager’s current access and permit rules, including the intended entry point.
  3. Share your route and expected return time with someone, and choose a lower-exposure alternative if conditions change.
